# Content Guide

## Voice and level

Use plain, calm, beginner-friendly language. Explain why a decision is safer. Do not shame learners or promise perfect protection.

## Content rules

- Use fictional organizations and reserved/example domains.
- Label pattern-based real-world material separately from fictional training examples.
- Never say that HTTPS, a known logo, spelling quality, or one red flag proves legitimacy or phishing.
- Treat urgency, sender identity, domain, context, request, and destination as combined evidence.
- Recommend independent verification through a known application, bookmark, phone number, or organizational channel.
- Do not add credential fields, active links to suspicious domains, executable attachments, or offensive instructions.

## Updating exercises

Edit `js/challenges.js` or `js/quiz.js`. Each item includes an answer and a short reason. Keep explanations specific to the evidence shown.
